# Same Question, Seven Engines: What Each AI Search Engine Cited — and Working Hypotheses for Targeting Each

**A source-forensics case study for Generative Engine Optimization (GEO)**

Canlah Research · Singapore · 2026-08-30 · v1.0 · License: CC BY 4.0 · Data, prompts, and classification code in this repository

---

## TL;DR

On one night (2026-08-29) we asked seven search/answer engines the same commercial question — *"best GEO company in Singapore"*, in English and Chinese — captured every result or citation they returned (116 citation records; 80 raw URLs, 71 unique pages after normalization), attempted to fetch every cited page, classified each page with a frozen, published codebook, and compared what we observed against each engine's **documented** retrieval mechanism (56 sourced claims, each re-checked by an adversarial verifier before publication: 53 confirmed, 3 kept only in softened form, 0 refuted).

In this run, **the engines barely overlapped on sources — no page was cited by more than 4 of the 7 engines, and mean pairwise top-10 domain Jaccard was 0.171 (CN) / 0.091 (EN) — yet largely the same vendor brands recurred across their answers.** If that pattern holds beyond this case, "optimizing for AI search" as a single channel is a category error: each engine has a distinct sourcing mechanism, and each mechanism suggests a different move.

This is **one query, one night, one locale** — a forensic case study with a method you can rerun for your own category, not a statistical survey. Read §2 before quoting any number.

| Engine | Surface | Dominant source shape (this run) | Working hypothesis to target it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google organic SERP | Traditional search | vendor service pages (13/18) | an exact-phrase, city-anchored service page may be the primary entry asset. |
| Google AI Mode | Generative answer (consumer) | service pages + entity/Places material | entity-layer work (GBP, reviews, locality) may matter more here than page-shape work. |
| Gemini + Google Search grounding (API) | Generative answer (API) | geospatial/GIS pages (query misread, 16/16) | explicit disambiguation (full phrase + AI-search context terms) may be a precondition for appearing at all. |

## 1. Why this exists

"AI search optimization" is usually sold as one channel, as if ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, Gemini, and the retrieval APIs behind agent stacks drew on one common source layer. In the run we measured, they did not. If you are deciding where to spend content and entity-building effort, the useful question is per-engine: **what shape of page did it cite, what does its documentation say about why, and what would you test first?** This document is our answer for one case, published with all data and code so that any company — including our competitors and their clients — can rerun the analysis for their own category.

## 2. Method summary — and what this study is *not*

1. **Probe** — 2026-08-29, single night, Singapore egress. One question, two languages, seven engines. Full per-engine call specification and verbatim prompts: [`method/probes.md`](method/probes.md). An eighth probe (Serper.dev) failed on account credits and is excluded.
2. **Capture** — up to the first 10 organic results (Google returned 9 per language), or the full ordered citation list for answer engines. 116 citation records. Raw engine outputs: [`data/raw_engine_responses.json`](data/raw_engine_responses.json).
3. **Fetch** — attempted for all cited URLs the same night. Of 80 raw URLs, 77 have an archived capture (73 direct, 4 recovered via a secondary retrieval channel; a few captures are bot-challenge interstitials rather than article content — visible in the `fetched_title` column); 3 yielded nothing and carry `retrieval_status=unfetched`. SHA-256 hashes of every archived capture are in [`data/sources.csv`](data/sources.csv); the raw HTML archive is retained internally and available to bona-fide replicators on request.
4. **Classify** — every fetched page was labeled by a deterministic script with a frozen codebook ([`scripts/classify.py`](scripts/classify.py)): vendor service page / self-published listicle (publisher appears in its own ranked list) / third-party listicle / directory-media. No manual overrides. Known limitation: the entity lexicon covers the GEO-marketing cluster, so self-ranked lists inside the geospatial/GIS cluster are conservatively typed as vendor pages.
5. **Reverse-engineer** — an independent model analyzed the evidence folder and produced per-engine observations, labeled *observed* vs *inferred*; the four most surprising claims were manually re-checked against raw HTML.
6. **Document-layer cross-check** — for each engine we collected up to 8 claims about its retrieval mechanism from primary sources (official docs, engineering blogs, credible research), then an adversarial verifier opened every cited source and judged whether it supports the claim. Verdicts with reasons: [`data/claim_verification.json`](data/claim_verification.json).

**What this study is not.** It is not a statistical survey (n = 1 query × 1 night × 1 locale × 2 languages); AI-answer output is known to vary run-to-run, so every number is one snapshot. Result lists (traditional engines) and citation lists (answer engines) are related but not identical units. Documented mechanisms cannot prove that a mechanism *caused* this run's output — alignment between the two is stated as consistency, not causation. The ChatGPT consumer probe ran on a logged-in account whose personalization/memory could not be fully ruled out; its observations are corroborated where possible but its specific vendor picks should not be quoted as neutral (see also §6 — that run cited our own site). And several cited "rankings" are published by ranked vendors; we label these rather than exclude them, because in this run the engines did not exclude them either.

## 3. Findings from this run

1. **No shared source layer.** Across 71 unique pages, none was cited by ≥5 of 7 engines; the three best-covered pages reached 4 each. Mean pairwise top-10 domain Jaccard: 0.171 (CN), 0.091 (EN). Matrices: [`data/similarity.json`](data/similarity.json).
2. **Sources diverged; brands recurred.** Engines cited largely different URLs while naming overlapping vendor sets (brand-level Jaccard between answer engines: 0–0.33, also in `similarity.json`). One hypothesis consistent with the data: a **second-order mention network** — many different publishers (listicles, directories, competitor pages) enumerate the same entities, so entity-level presence travels across engines even when no single URL does.
3. **Self-published "Best X" listicles were taken up by exactly three surfaces in this run:** OpenAI web_search (3/9 citations), ChatGPT consumer (6/17), and Google organic (4/18 — top-10 entries, never #1). Zero uptake by Exa, Tavily, Google AI Mode, or Gemini. No engine visibly filtered the conflict of interest: every self-listicle we captured ranks its publisher first, and was cited anyway.
4. **Structured data correlated with citation, and guarantees nothing.** 17/17 ChatGPT-cited and 16/18 Google-cited records carried JSON-LD — but AI Mode and Gemini also cited schema-free pages, bot-challenged pages, and one 404. Google's own documentation disclaims any guarantee. Treat schema as hygiene.
5. **Word-sense disambiguation decided one engine entirely.** Gemini grounding resolved "GEO" to geospatial/GIS in both languages (16/16 sources), including pages updated days before the probe — freshness did not rescue the lost word-sense. If your category term is ambiguous, resolving the ambiguity on-page (full phrase, AI-search context terms) looks like a precondition for competing at all.

## 4. Per-engine: what it cited, what its documentation says, and what to test

### 4.1 Google organic SERP — Traditional search

> **If you target this engine — working hypothesis from this run:** In this run, exact-match locally-anchored vendor service pages took 13/18 citation slots. Hypothesis to test: an exact-phrase, city-anchored service page may be the primary entry asset.

**Observed source profile** (this case, n=18 citation records): 13 vendor service pages · 4 self-published listicles · 1 third-party listicle · 0 directories/media. 14 unique pages (slash-normalized), of which 7 were cited by no other engine in this run.

**How it sources — documented mechanism.** Claims below come from primary documentation; each was re-checked by an adversarial verifier that opened the cited source (verdicts + reasons in [`data/claim_verification.json`](data/claim_verification.json)):

- The most basic relevance signal is literal keyword overlap between page content and the query. ([Google — How Search Works: Ranking results](https://www.google.com/search/howsearchworks/how-search-works/ranking-results/) — “The most basic signal that information is relevant is when content contains the same keywords as your search query.”)
- Google detects local intent in queries and serves geographically relevant businesses without explicit location words. ([Google — How Search Works: Ranking results](https://www.google.com/search/howsearchworks/how-search-works/ranking-results/) — “Our systems can also recognize many queries have a local intent. So when you search for 'pizza,' you get results about nearby businesses.”)
- Core ranking rewards people-first content, defined against content made to manipulate rankings. ([Google Search Central — Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) — “People-first content means content that's created primarily for people, and not to manipulate search engine rankings.”)
- Automated systems look for signals of E-E-A-T (experience, expertise, authoritativeness, trustworthiness) to prioritize content. ([Google Search Central — Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) — “Our systems aim to prioritize those that seem most helpful by identifying factors that can help determine which content demonstrates aspects of experience, expertise, authoritativeness, and trustworthiness.”)
- A dedicated reviews system evaluates 'best X' ranked lists and rewards original research over recycled summaries. ([Google Search Central — Google Search's reviews system](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/reviews-system) — “The reviews system aims to better reward high quality reviews, which is content that provides insightful analysis and original research. ... Reviews can be head-to-head comparisons, or ranked-lists of recommendations.”)
- Structured data confers rich-result eligibility only; Google explicitly disclaims any guarantee of display or ranking effect. ([Google Search Central — Structured data general guidelines](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/sd-policies) — “Google does not guarantee that your structured data will show up in search results, even if your page is marked up correctly.”)
- Freshness is applied per-query ('query deserves freshness'), not as a universal ranking requirement. ([Google Search Central — A guide to Google Search ranking systems](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ranking-systems-guide) — “Various 'query deserves freshness' systems designed to show fresher content for queries where it would be expected.”)
- The site diversity system caps how many listings one domain can hold in top results. ([Google Search Central — A guide to Google Search ranking systems](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ranking-systems-guide) — “Generally won't show more than two web page listings from the same site in top results.”)

**Observed vs. documented (this run):** Strong alignment on relevance and locality: all 18/18 engine-displayed result titles contained both "Singapore" and GEO/Generative-Engine semantics, matching Google's documented "same keywords" relevance signal and local-intent handling — this is consistent with why vendor service pages with exact-match titles took 13/18 slots. The structured-data picture also matches documentation: 16/18 winners carried JSON-LD (correlation), yet a Cloudflare-blocked page and schema-light pages still ranked, consistent with Google's explicit "no guarantee" stance. Self-serving "best of" listicles reaching positions 2/5/8 but never #1 is directionally consistent with the reviews system rewarding original research over self-promotion, though Google nowhere documents a conflict-of-interest filter. The main tension: documented people-first/E-E-A-T rhetoric versus an observed SERP dominated by vendor marketing pages — on low-competition niche queries, literal relevance matching visibly outweighs quality systems.

**Targeting hypotheses** — priority is the authors' judgment of what to test first, not a measured effect size:

| # | Action to test | Evidence basis | Test priority | Why |
|---|--------|----------|--------|-----|
| 1 | Build a dedicated service page whose title tag, H1, and URL slug contain the exact phrase 'Generative Engine Optimization' plus the city/country name (e.g. 'GEO Agency Singapore'). | observed | high | 13/18 observed citations were vendor service pages, and 18/18 winning titles matched query terms literally — Google documents keyword overlap as the most basic relevance signal. |
| 2 | Answer the superlative query verbatim on-page: an H2 like 'Who is the best GEO agency in Singapore?' followed by a direct answer paragraph naming your firm. | observed | high | OC Digital used exactly this question-answer structure and took position #1 in both Chinese and English SERPs in our experiment. |
| 3 | Establish local-entity signals: Singapore address and phone on-page, Organization/LocalBusiness markup, and a Google Business Profile, so local-intent processing resolves you as an in-market entity. | documented | high | Google documents automatic local-intent detection; every winning result was a Singapore-anchored entity, and Google's AI surfaces layered in Places data for the same query. |
| 4 | Publish a self-hosted ranked listicle ('10 Best GEO Agencies in Singapore 2026') as a secondary asset — but include a disclosed methodology, first-hand test evidence, and genuinely useful competitor comparisons. | observed | medium | Self-listicles entered organic top 10 (positions 2, 5, 8) but never won #1; Google's reviews system explicitly evaluates ranked lists and appears to favor original research and first-hand evidence. |
| 5 | Add FAQPage/Article/Service JSON-LD for machine parseability, treating it as an eligibility and extraction aid, not a ranking lever. | observed | medium | 16/18 winning records carried non-empty JSON-LD (12 with FAQ/Answer types), but Google explicitly states markup guarantees nothing — treat the correlation as hygiene, not causation. |
| 6 | Get listed in independent directories and third-party agency roundups so your entity occupies SERP slots beyond your own domain's two-listing cap. | documented | medium | Google's site diversity system limits one site to ~2 top listings; third-party listicles (Digital Agency Network, GenOptima) held additional top-10 positions in our data. |
| 7 | Prioritize exact topical match over year-stamping: keep content current, but don't rely on '2026' in titles as the entry ticket. | observed | low | Only 4/18 winning titles contained '2026'; Google documents freshness as query-dependent ('query deserves freshness'), and this commercial query did not demand it. |

**Not supported by this run** (practices that showed no uptake or are contradicted by documentation — not proof they never work):

- Treating schema markup as a ranking guarantee: Google explicitly disclaims any guarantee, and in our data pages behind Cloudflare challenges or with no JSON-LD still ranked on title/entity relevance alone.
- Ranking yourself #1 in your own 'best agencies' listicle and expecting the top organic position: all three observed self-listicles were capped at positions 2, 5, and 8 — the format gets you in, not on top.
- Stuffing the current year into titles as a freshness hack: 14/18 winning results had no '2026' in the title; exact topical and geographic match appeared to beat recency on this query.
- Letting unreviewed internal text into indexable templates: one ranked page (Not for external distribution' in its indexed content — Google indexes whatever ships, including reputation-damaging draft copy.
- Assuming a page your own fetcher can't read can't rank: First Page Digital ranked top-10 in both languages while serving only a Cloudflare 'Just a moment...' wall to our crawler — Googlebot access, not third-party-crawler access, is what counts for classic SERP (though it may cost you citations in AI engines).

### 4.2 Google AI Mode — Generative answer (consumer)

> **If you target this engine — working hypothesis from this run:** In this run it surfaced business-entity material (Business Profile cards, ratings) alongside pages, and cited no listicles. Hypothesis to test: entity-layer work (GBP, reviews, locality) may matter more here than page-shape work.

**Observed source profile** (this case, n=17 citation records): 12 vendor service pages · 0 self-published listicles · 0 third-party listicles · 4 directories/media · 1 unfetched (no page-type assigned). 16 unique pages (slash-normalized), of which 10 were cited by no other engine in this run.

**How it sources — documented mechanism.** Claims below come from primary documentation; each was re-checked by an adversarial verifier that opened the cited source (verdicts + reasons in [`data/claim_verification.json`](data/claim_verification.json)):

- AI Mode decomposes the user question into multiple parallel sub-searches across different data sources (query fan-out), so cited pages come from many sub-query SERPs, not one ranking. ([Google (official blog), 'Expanding AI Overviews and introducing AI Mode'](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/ai-mode-search/) — “It uses a 'query fan-out' technique, issuing multiple related searches concurrently across subtopics and multiple data sources”)
- Beyond web pages, AI Mode grounds answers in Google's structured real-time systems — Knowledge Graph, real-world (local/business) info, and the Shopping Graph — i.e., entity data is a first-class source alongside web content. ([Google (official blog), 'Expanding AI Overviews and introducing AI Mode'](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/ai-mode-search/) — “tap into fresh, real-time sources like the Knowledge Graph, info about the real world, and shopping data for billions of products”)
- Google's VP of Search describes AI Mode as literally re-running Google searches as a backend tool for each generated sub-question, with Google's internal real-time systems (Finance, flights, shopping catalog) wired in. ([Search Engine Journal, quoting Robby Stein (Google VP of Product, Search)](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/query-fan-out-technique-in-ai-mode-new-details-from-google/552532/) — “it may think of a bunch of questions like great restaurants, great bars ... and it'll start Googling basically”)
- Officially, no special markup, AI files, or optimizations gate inclusion; the only technical bar is being indexed and snippet-eligible, and standard SEO best practices apply. ([Google Search Central documentation, 'AI Features and Your Website'](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) — “There are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, nor other special optimizations necessary.”)
- Google explicitly says its AI features surface a broader, more varied link set than classic results for the same query — which predicts low overlap with the traditional SERP. ([Google Search Central documentation, 'AI Features and Your Website'](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) — “display a wider and more diverse set of helpful links”)
- Ahrefs (863K keywords, 4M AI Overview URLs) found only 38% of AI Overview citations rank in the head query's top 10 (down from ~76%), with ~31% ranking beyond position 100 — consistent with fan-out sourcing. ([Ahrefs blog, AI Overview citations study](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-citations-top-10) — “38% of pages cited in AI Overviews also rank in the top 10”)
- For local queries, AI Mode builds answers around business entities: cards with star ratings, review volumes, hours and pricing pulled from Google Business Profiles, supplemented by review sites and unstructured web citations. ([Whitespark, 'Guide to Google AI Mode for Local Businesses'](https://whitespark.ca/guides/whitesparks-guide-to-googles-ai-mode-for-local-businesses/) — “summaries about businesses and information about star ratings, review volumes, pricing, hours of operation, and other basic facts pulled from Google Business Profiles or other sources”)
- Pew Research (68,879 real user queries) found AI summary citations concentrate on a few platforms (Wikipedia/YouTube/Reddit = 15% of links) and links inside summaries are clicked in only 1% of visits. ([Pew Research Center, AI summaries browsing-behavior study](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/) — “Google users who encountered an AI summary also rarely clicked on a link in the summary itself. This occurred in just 1% of all visits”)

**Observed vs. documented (this run):** Our n=1 forensic run matches the documented mechanism closely. AI Mode cited 0 listicles while the classic SERP for the same query cited 5 — exactly what query fan-out plus "wider and more diverse set of helpful links" predicts, and consistent with Ahrefs' finding that only 38% of AI citations come from the head query's top 10. Our four Google "searchviewer" Place cards (OOm 4.8/310 reviews, Impossible 4.9/208, AD GUYS 4.9/350) confirm the documented Knowledge-Graph/Business-Profile grounding: the engine appears to fix local business entities first, then gathers service pages and directories as supporting evidence. Only 10/17 citations had JSON-LD, matching "no special markup needed." Undocumented anywhere: citations frequently resolving to root domains rather than the specific article the displayed title implies, which hurts auditability.

**Targeting hypotheses** — priority is the authors' judgment of what to test first, not a measured effect size:

| # | Action to test | Evidence basis | Test priority | Why |
|---|--------|----------|--------|-----|
| 1 | Treat your Google Business Profile as a primary ranking surface: correct category, complete services, active review generation, and owner responses — AI Mode renders Place cards (rating + review count) as first-class citations before any web page. | observed | high | In our run 4/9 English citations were Google Place-viewer cards keyed to GBP entities (4.8-4.9 stars, 200-350 reviews); Whitespark documents GBP as the data source for these cards. |
| 2 | Build one canonical service page whose title, H1 and URL exactly match the commercial entity query ('Generative Engine Optimization Singapore' / 'GEO Agency Singapore'), with sections on methodology, platforms covered, measurement and pricing. | observed | high | 12/17 AI Mode citations in our run were vendor service pages; it cited zero listicles, so your own precise service page is the main web-content slot you can win. |
| 3 | Author content to answer fan-out sub-questions, not just the head query: add distinct, extractable sections for 'how GEO differs from SEO', 'GEO pricing in Singapore', 'how to measure AI citations', each able to rank for its own sub-query. | documented | high | Google documents that AI Mode issues multiple concurrent sub-searches and cites from those SERPs; Ahrefs shows 62% of citations do NOT rank top-10 for the head query — sub-query SERPs are the real entry points. |
| 4 | Make your homepage/root domain a self-sufficient entity statement (who you are, what GEO services, where, trust numbers) because AI Mode citations often resolve to the root domain rather than the deep article. | observed | medium | We observed Digital Agency Network, Tenten, Semrush and ifeng citations all landing on root pages while displaying article-like titles; a thin homepage wastes that citation. |
| 5 | Verify Googlebot-render access and snippet eligibility: no Cloudflare challenge for Google's crawlers, no max-snippet/nosnippet restrictions, key claims in plain HTML text. | documented | medium | Google's only stated technical bar is 'indexed and eligible to be shown with a snippet'; several cited competitors were unreadable to third-party auditors but remained visible to Google — don't rely on that luck. |
| 6 | Seed review text (not just star counts) that names your service category: encourage clients to mention 'generative engine optimization' / 'AI search' in Google and third-party reviews. | documented | medium | Whitespark documents AI Mode compositing review content from Google and sites like Yelp into business summaries; review text becomes retrievable evidence about what you do. |
| 7 | Get listed in independent agency directories (e.g., Digital Agency Network, Semrush Agency Partners) with a complete profile. | observed | medium | 4/17 AI Mode citations were directory/media pages — the only non-vendor web sources it used; directories are the surviving third-party validation channel once listicles are filtered out. |
| 8 | Disambiguate 'GEO' aggressively on-page: always pair the acronym with the full phrase 'Generative Engine Optimization' plus context terms (ChatGPT, AI Overviews, AI search) in title, H1 and first paragraph. | observed | medium | AI Mode itself pulled in one geospatial-industry directory (Ensun) on the English query; sibling engine Gemini drifted 16/16 citations into GIS companies — acronym ambiguity is a real retrieval failure mode. |

**Not supported by this run** (practices that showed no uptake or are contradicted by documentation — not proof they never work):

- Publishing a self-serving 'Best GEO agencies' listicle that ranks yourself #1: effective on ChatGPT/GPT-search in our run, but AI Mode cited 0/17 listicles of any kind — this asset class did not surface here at all.
- Treating JSON-LD/schema as an entry ticket: only 10/17 AI Mode citations had any JSON-LD (lowest of the web engines we tested), and Google explicitly states no special markup or AI files are required.
- Optimizing only for top-10 rank on the head query: Ahrefs found just 38% of AI Overview citations rank top-10, and our AI Mode citation set shared only 6/16 pages with the classic SERP for the identical query.
- Stamping '2026' or fresh dates on pages as a visibility lever: in our run AI Mode cited undated homepages and business cards alongside dated pages — freshness was not a gating signal for this query class.
- Website-only local strategy that ignores the Google Business Profile: AI Mode anchored its English answer on Place-entity cards; a competitor with a blocked website but a strong GBP (4.9 stars, 350 reviews) still got cited.

### 4.3 Gemini + Google Search grounding (API) — Generative answer (API)

> **If you target this engine — working hypothesis from this run:** In this run it resolved 'GEO' to geospatial/GIS in both languages (16/16 sources). Hypothesis to test: explicit disambiguation (full phrase + AI-search context terms) may be a precondition for appearing at all.

**Observed source profile** (this case, n=16 citation records): 13 vendor service pages · 0 self-published listicles · 0 third-party listicles · 3 directories/media. 14 unique pages (slash-normalized), of which 13 were cited by no other engine in this run.

**How it sources — documented mechanism.** Claims below come from primary documentation; each was re-checked by an adversarial verifier that opened the cited source (verdicts + reasons in [`data/claim_verification.json`](data/claim_verification.json)):

- The model itself decides whether to search: it evaluates the prompt and only triggers Google Search if it judges retrieval would help. ([Google AI for Developers — Grounding with Google Search (Gemini API docs)](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/generate-content/google-search) — “The model analyzes the prompt and determines if a Google Search can improve the answer.”)
- Search queries are generated by the model, not passed through verbatim from the user prompt — so intent disambiguation (e.g. GEO vs geospatial) happens inside the model's query-formulation step, with no documented safeguard. ([Google AI for Developers — Grounding with Google Search (Gemini API docs)](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/generate-content/google-search) — “If needed, the model automatically generates one or multiple search queries and executes them.”)
- The model-generated queries are exposed in the API response as webSearchQueries, making the query-rewriting step directly auditable. ([Firebase AI Logic — Grounding with Google Search](https://firebase.google.com/docs/ai-logic/grounding-google-search) — “An array of the search queries sent to Google Search. This information is useful for debugging and understanding the model's reasoning process.”)
- Citations are returned as groundingChunks (web sources with uri and title) plus groundingSupports that map answer text spans to those sources. ([Google AI for Developers — Grounding with Google Search (Gemini API docs)](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/generate-content/google-search) — “Each chunk links a text segment (defined by startIndex and endIndex) to one or more groundingChunkIndices.”)
- Cited URIs are not the source's canonical URL but Google-operated redirect links on vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com — a widely reported, unresolved developer pain point. ([googleapis/python-genai GitHub issue #1512](https://github.com/googleapis/python-genai/issues/1512) — “all the URLs in the metadata are redirect links to vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com”)
- The legacy dynamic-retrieval mode scored each prompt for grounding benefit against a configurable threshold (default 0.3); on current models this is superseded by the google_search tool where the model decides. ([Google Developers Blog — Gemini API and Google AI Studio now offer Grounding with Google Search](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/gemini-api-and-ai-studio-now-offer-grounding-with-google-search/) — “The dynamic retrieval configuration assigns the prompt a prediction score, which is a floating point value between 0 and 1.”)
- Current Gemini models (2.0+, incl. 2.5-flash) use the google_search tool; google_search_retrieval with dynamic threshold is legacy-only. ([Google AI for Developers — Grounding with Google Search (current docs)](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/google-search) — “Older models use a google_search_retrieval tool. For all current models, use the google_search tool as shown in the examples.”)
- Independent instrumentation of Gemini grounding payloads found the model receives only SERP-level data (title, URL, snippet, query) per result — not full page content. ([Dejan.ai — Hacking Gemini (grounding payload analysis)](https://dejan.ai/blog/hacking-gemini/) — “In short, Gemini sees url, title, query and snippet. It then generates that summary from it.”)

**Observed vs. documented (this run):** Documentation is consistent with the failure point we observed: the model — not the user — formulates the search queries ("automatically generates one or multiple search queries"), and no Google document describes any disambiguation or query-verification safeguard. Our n=1 forensic run showed gemini-2.5-flash resolving "GEO company in Singapore" into geospatial/GIS intent in both Chinese and English (16/16 citations geospatial; 13/14 cited pages exclusive to this engine), producing an internally coherent but globally wrong candidate set — exactly what an unaudited query-rewrite step would predict. Snippet-only grounding context (Dejan payload analysis) is consistent with why page content never corrected the misread intent. Low JSON-LD incidence (6/16) matches a search-index-driven, not schema-driven, pipeline. Redirect-style citation URIs matched docs and the GitHub issue precisely.

**Targeting hypotheses** — priority is the authors' judgment of what to test first, not a measured effect size:

| # | Action to test | Evidence basis | Test priority | Why |

- Tavily's stated design objective is maximizing information density per millisecond of retrieval. *(WEAK: only the quoted core is verified)* ([Tavily Engineering Blog — How we built the fastest web search](https://www.tavily.com/blog/how-we-built-the-fastest-web-search-in-the-world) — “From crawl selection, ranking, and synthesis to output structure, every design choice is guided by a single question: Does this increase the Information Density per millisecond we deliver?”)
- Independent measurement finds Tavily surfaces largely the same domain set as Google but reorders it with its own LLM-tuned relevance model — Google rank order does not transfer. ([cloro.dev technical analysis — Tavily vs Google Search](https://cloro.dev/blog/tavily-vs-google-search/) — “80% of Google's top-10 domains also appeared somewhere in Tavily's results ... top-3 domain overlap was just 0.25 and rank correlation near zero.”)

**Observed vs. documented (this run):** Strong alignment. Our n=1 run (19 citations, zh+en "best GEO company in Singapore") gave Tavily the widest page-type spread of all seven engines — 11 vendor service pages, 3 third-party listicles, 5 directory/media/social, zero self-listicles — exactly what topic=general's "wide range of sources" and the pure query-content relevance score predict: no page-type preference, no intent gate. Loose topical filtering (generic SEO listicles and global, non-Singapore lists leaking in) matches ranking by lexical-semantic relevance alone. Facebook/Instagram one-word stubs and Cloudflare-blocked pages appearing in results shows ranking does not require successful content extraction — consistent with docs' silence on any fetchability gate. One conflict: docs claim "highest relevance" curation, but our data shows the weakest precision of the marketing-semantics engines.

**Targeting hypotheses** — priority is the authors' judgment of what to test first, not a measured effect size:

| # | Action to test | Evidence basis | Test priority | Why |
|---|--------|----------|--------|-----|
| 1 | Put the exact query surface in title, H1, and URL slug: "Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) Company Singapore", not creative brand-first titles — Tavily ranks by a query-to-content relevance score, and 19/19 of its citations in our run were pages whose titles lexically matched the query frame. | observed | high | Docs state ranking is a relevance score between query and content with no other documented signal; our citation set was dominated by exact-match titles. |
| 2 | Write chunk-sized, self-contained answer passages (2-4 sentences that alone answer "who is the best GEO company in Singapore and why") near the top of the page, since basic search_depth returns short snippets reranked by relevance, and include_answer is synthesized only from those returned chunks. | documented | high | Documented chunk-based retrieval means one high-scoring passage, not whole-page authority, did best in this run the citation and feeds the generated answer. |
| 3 | Get listed in third-party directories and media roundups (Digital Agency Network-type pages, industry press): 8/19 Tavily citations were third-party listicles or directory/media pages — the highest such share among the engines we tested. | observed | high | Tavily's broad candidate set actively pulls directory and media pages that most other engines skipped, so off-site listings are a real Tavily surface. |
| 4 | Maintain a dedicated, plainly-scoped GEO service page per market — 11/19 Tavily citations were vendor service pages, and Tavily cited them without requiring listicle structure or comparison format. | observed | high | Vendor self-descriptions were the single largest cited type; no ranking-page format is needed to be retrievable. |
| 5 | Year-stamp titles and keep a real recent published/updated date: 6/19 Tavily result titles carried "2026", and the API exposes time_range/start_date filters plus topic=news with published_date metadata that agents commonly apply. | documented | medium | Documented freshness filters mean date-filtered agent queries silently drop undated or stale pages; year-in-title also matches common "best X 2026" query phrasing. |
| 6 | Serve fully rendered, crawler-readable HTML with no Cloudflare challenge or JS-only content: blocked pages appeared in Tavily results as empty shells in this run, contributing a URL but zero extractable text to the LLM answer. | observed | high | Since include_answer is generated from extracted content, an unextractable page wastes its ranking slot — you get counted but never quoted. |
| 7 | Also compete on adjacent generic surfaces (plain "SEO agency Singapore" listicles, global GEO roundups): Tavily's loose intent constraint let generic SEO lists and non-Singapore global lists into the GEO answer set, so placements there leak into GEO queries. | inferred | medium | Weak topical filtering observed in our run means adjacent-topic placements have real spillover into GEO-intent retrievals on Tavily. |

**Not supported by this run** (practices that showed no uptake or are contradicted by documentation — not proof they never work):

- Self-published "we are #1" listicles on your own domain: 0/19 Tavily citations in our run were self-listicles, versus uptake by GPT Search (3/9) and ChatGPT consumer (6/17) — this content shape did not transfer to Tavily.
- Treating JSON-LD/schema markup as a Tavily ranking lever: 6/19 cited records had no JSON-LD at all, and Tavily's documentation never mentions structured data as a retrieval or ranking signal.
- Optimizing for Google rank order and assuming it carries over: independent measurement found top-3 domain overlap of 0.25 and rank correlation near zero between Google and Tavily, despite ~80% domain-set overlap — position, not presence, is what fails to transfer.
- Gating content behind Cloudflare challenges or JS-only rendering: your URL may still surface, but with no extractable text you contribute nothing to chunks or the include_answer synthesis — a wasted citation.
- Relying on Tavily's engine-side filtering as quality control for your category: it applied the loosest intent constraint of the seven engines we tested (generic SEO lists, global lists, social stubs all admitted), so category pollution around your brand terms flows straight into agent answers.
